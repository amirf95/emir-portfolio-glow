Conclusions & Commentary

The survey results paint a clear picture: online shopping is deeply embedded in daily life, especially among younger and college-educated demographics. The 90% purchase rate among respondents is consistent with national and global trends reported by major research organizations.

1. E-Commerce adoption is nearly universal among young adults

All 12 respondents in the 18–25 age group had purchased online, and four of them shop weekly. This group also skews toward mobile-first platforms like Trendyol and Amazon Prime. The Pew Research Center has consistently found that younger adults lead in online shopping adoption.

"Some 79% of Americans say they buy things online… those in the 18–29 age group are among the most likely to do so." — Pew Research Center, "Online Shopping and E-Commerce" report

2. Monthly purchasing is the dominant pattern

Half of all online shoppers in the survey purchase once a month, which aligns with subscription-driven and repeat-purchase behavior fueled by platforms like Amazon Prime. Weekly shoppers (22%) tend to be in the 18–25 bracket and report fashion and electronics as their primary categories.

"Global e-commerce sales are projected to surpass $6 trillion, driven by recurring purchases from loyalty program members and mobile app users." — eMarketer, Global E-Commerce Forecast

3. Security concerns remain the main barrier to purchasing

Both non-purchasers cited distrust of online payment systems as their primary reason for browsing without buying. Despite major advances in SSL encryption and buyer protection programs, trust remains a significant conversion barrier — particularly for older respondents.

"Concerns about privacy and security continue to discourage a segment of internet users from making online transactions, even as overall adoption grows." — Pew Internet & American Life Project (pewinternet.org)

4. Amazon dominates, but regional platforms are competitive

Amazon was the top choice (40%), but regional Turkish platforms Trendyol (25%) and Hepsiburada (15%) together account for 40% of favorite sites, reflecting the strength of local market players. ClickZ research notes that localization — local language, local payment methods, and fast domestic shipping — is a key driver of regional platform success over global giants.

"Regional e-commerce platforms have gained significant market share by offering localized payment options, same-day delivery, and culturally relevant product selections." — ClickZ, E-Commerce Market Analysis

5. Education level correlates with purchase frequency

All four weekly shoppers held at least some college education, while the once-a-year shoppers were more evenly spread across education levels. Higher education appears to correlate with greater digital comfort and more frequent online transactions, which is consistent with findings reported by E-Commerce Times.

"Consumers with higher education levels are more likely to shop online frequently and spend more per transaction, partly due to higher income and greater familiarity with digital tools." — E-Commerce Times (ecommercetimes.com)
