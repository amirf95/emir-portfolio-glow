A Century of History

Club Africain was officially founded on October 4, 1920, in Tunis, Tunisia — born from the spirit of "Stade Africain," an earlier sports association active since 1915. When Stade Africain was dissolved in 1918, its players, colors, and soul were carried forward into what became Club Africain.

Wearing red and white, Club Africain rose to become one of the two dominant forces in Tunisian football — the other being their fierce rival, Espérance Sportive de Tunis. The club's nickname is "Al Ifriki" — simply, "The African" — a name that carries the weight of continental ambition and cultural identity.

In 1991, Club Africain etched their name into continental history by winning the CAF African Cup of Champions Clubs — the precursor to today's CAF Champions League — becoming the first Tunisian club ever to lift the continental crown. They defeated Uganda's Nakivubo Villa SC 7–3 on aggregate in the final. The following year, they won the Afro-Asian Club Championship (1992), cementing their status as Africa's finest.

The Stadium

Stade Olympique de Radès — Primary Venue

Club Africain play their home matches at the Stade Olympique de Radès, Tunisia's national stadium with a capacity of approximately 60,000 supporters. It is one of the largest and most modern stadiums in Africa, shared with the Tunisian national team.

On derby night, the Stade de Radès becomes one of the most electric atmospheres in all of African football. The Virage Nord — the north curve — belongs to the red and white ultras. The noise, the smoke, the tifos: this is where the Ultras Africain Winners write history every matchday.

El Menzah & Zouiten

The club also uses Stade Olympique d'El Menzah (capacity ~39,858) as a secondary venue, and the historic Stade Chedly Zouiten holds a place in the club's earlier history.

The Derby Tunisois

No rivalry in Tunisian football — perhaps in all of African football — burns as intensely as the Derby Tunisois: Club Africain vs. Espérance Sportive de Tunis (EST).

Two clubs, one city, one stadium. On derby day, Tunis splits in two. The red and white of Al Ifriki against the blood and gold of Taraji. Streets go quiet before kickoff. The result shapes an entire week — sometimes an entire season.

The 2026 title-winning moment encapsulates everything: a league championship sealed with a goal in the 104th minute of the final-day derby — beating Espérance on their shared pitch to claim the championship. There is no more fitting way for Club Africain to win.

"When the derby comes, the city stops. When Africain wins the derby, the city sings."
