Tunisia's Ultra Scene

Tunisia has the richest ultra tradition in the Arab world — a heritage stretching back to 1995 when African Winners invented the form. Today, four organized ultra groups define the identity of Tunisian football's biggest clubs. Each group has its own culture, its own stadium stand, its own visual language — but all share the same founding principles brought to Tunisia by African Winners three decades ago.

01
African Winners
Club Africain • Tunis

The original. Founded in 1995, African Winners was the first ultra group in Tunisia and the entire Arab world. They introduced tifo culture, organized chanting, and the concept of the ultra curve to North African football. Every ultra group in the Arab world exists in the shadow of what African Winners built in the north stand of Stade de Radès.

Their name references Club Africain's historic 1991 CAF Champions League title — they are the supporters of Africa's continental winners, and they carry that identity with pride.

02
Ultras L'Emkachkhines
Espérance Sportive de Tunis • Tunis

Founded in 2002, Ultras L'Emkachkhines (ULE) are the organized ultra wing of Espérance Sportive de Tunis — Club Africain's great rivals. The name means "those who are crazy about it" in Tunisian Arabic slang. They modeled their early structure on Italian ultra groups, specifically the Curva Sud of AS Roma, adopting fierce visual identity and sophisticated tifo design.

Their rivalry with African Winners in the north and south stands of Stade de Radès produces some of the most intense ultra atmospheres in African football. The ULE use blood-and-gold colors — the "Sang et Or" of Espérance — against the red and white of Africain.

Founded 2002 Espérance Sportive Blood & Gold Rivals of African Winners
03
Brigades Rouges
Étoile Sportive du Sahel • Sousse

The principal ultra group of Étoile Sportive du Sahel (ESS), the major club of Sousse on Tunisia's eastern coast. The name "Brigades Rouges" — Red Brigades — carries deliberate historical resonance, invoking both the political passion of the name and the club's red identity. They occupy the stand at Stade Olympique de Sousse.

ESS is one of Tunisia's most successful clubs and a serious continental competitor — their ultra support reflects that ambition. Brigades Rouges are known for passionate vocal support and a strong presence on away trips, reinforcing the Sahel identity across Tunisian stadiums.

Étoile du Sahel Sousse Stade Olympique de Sousse
04
Fighters
Club Sportif Sfaxien • Sfax

The ultra group of Club Sportif Sfaxien (CSS), the flagship club of Sfax — Tunisia's second city and its economic capital. The name "Fighters" reflects both the combative spirit of ultra support and the identity of Sfax as a city that has always fought for its place against the dominance of Tunis-based clubs.

CSS is a historically significant club with multiple Tunisian titles and African campaigns. Their Fighters group has built a loyal support culture in a football environment dominated by the Tunis big two, which makes their dedication all the more admirable — they fight for recognition, for the south, for Sfax.

Winners 2005 — Wydad Casablanca

Wydad Athletic Club (WAC) is the great red club of Casablanca, Morocco — one of the most successful clubs in African football history, winners of multiple CAF Champions League titles. Their colors: red and white. Their identity: les Rouges de Casablanca.

In 2005, the year ultra culture formally arrived in Morocco, Wydad supporters organized themselves as Winners 2005. The name was not chosen by accident. They knew the tradition they were entering — a North African ultra world where the name "Winners" already carried weight, where Club Africain's African Winners had been flying that banner for a decade.

Winners 2005 have become one of the most decorated ultra groups in the world. They have been named World's Best Ultras four times by Ultras World magazine (2015, 2017, 2019, 2022). Their tifos are legendary — massive, creative, emotionally powerful. They have raised the standard of Moroccan and African ultra culture to a global level.

The Connection

The bond between the African Winners tradition and Winners 2005 is built on multiple layers:

The name. "Winners" — used by both groups, separated by a decade. In the North African ultra world, names carry history. Using "Winners" signals knowledge, respect, and lineage.

The colors. Club Africain and Wydad Casablanca both wear red and white. Two red clubs. Two red-and-white curves. Two groups who understand that red is not just a color — it is a commitment.

The continent. Both clubs have won the CAF Champions League. African football's greatest clubs, supported by African football's most passionate fans. The respect is mutual and earned.

The brotherhood. North African ultra culture has always been more porous than its European counterpart — connections across Tunisia, Morocco, and Algeria are natural, celebrated, and proudly displayed. When Winners 2005 and the African Winners world meet, they meet as peers who know the same songs in different dialects.

"Two red clubs. Two red curves. Two groups who chose the same word — Winners. In North Africa, that is not a coincidence. It is a conversation across a decade."

Wydad Athletic Club — A Portrait

Founded in 1937 in Casablanca, Wydad is one of the oldest and most decorated clubs in Morocco and Africa. They have won the CAF Champions League multiple times, including recent continental triumphs, and are perennial Moroccan league champions. Their stadium — the Stade Mohamed V in Casablanca — fills with red and white on matchday, mirroring the colors and passion of the north curve in Radès.

Casablanca is to Moroccan football what Tunis is to Tunisian football: the capital of passion, the city where a football match is never just a football match. It is identity. It is belonging. It is red, and it will always be red.
